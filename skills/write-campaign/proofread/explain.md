# Reading for the explanation, the section forms, the style and the campaign's own rules

Rules: every `purpose.`, `arrangement.`, `lead-summary.`, `sections.`, `style.` and `page.` rule, `facts.config-effects`, `coverage.observability`, `conventions.subsystem-entry`, the template `guidelines/page/page-template.md`, and every ban, mandatory feature, caution and rule of the campaign as the brief states them.

1. `--prefix style. --prefix arrangement. --prefix lead-summary. --prefix sections. --prefix page. --include-off` once: the mechanical sweeps whose policy is off; fix what the rule's exception column does not clear.
2. The `skim`, `openers` and `closers` views in sequence, as a reader who knows the kernel and not this subsystem: the sequence progresses through objects, states and relationships; every DETAILS heading is a declarative clause true of its section; a subsection that opens on a symbol, a count or a list, or whose first paragraph names an object and its members without saying why the kernel keeps it, gets its purpose sentence.
3. Every DETAILS subsection whole, with its source and figures: the reader can identify the current object, see why the operation or condition is needed, and carry its result into the next step from what the page has established; one subsection per phase or facet, never one per symbol; a vocabulary used in two senses is settled once near the model figure.
4. The lead, SUMMARY, the CAUTION, the H2 order, the route paragraph, the Kconfig overview (one row per option the prose names, none the prose never names), the recap paragraphs: the forms the rules give.
5. Paragraph and sentence shape against `arrangement.paragraphs` and `style.sentence-shape`; a list under DETAILS or SUMMARY is prose or a table.
6. The campaign's bans and rules in the authored words: the prose, the table cells, the figure labels and legends, never the C fences.
7. Every path, member, constant or branch that depends on a configuration option names the option where the dependence bites; the observability subsection lists the four classes with sites or names each as absent.
8. The last section, WEAKLY SUPPORTED CLAIMS, when the page has one:
   - each entry against the heading it names;
   - an entry you can close within the budget (a second basis, an excerpt within the page's subject, a scope condition written into the sentence) is closed and removed;
   - an entry about form is removed and reported under left;
   - a claim the page states on one check, on code it does not show or through a model figure's simplification, and does not list, gets its entry in the rule's form;
   - the section goes when its last entry goes.
