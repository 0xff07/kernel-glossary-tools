# Post fence subject

The first sentence after a source fence explains the behavior, condition, state or consequence its lines establish, with a subject the reader can identify without searching back across the fence. Use the established object or role when it is clear; name and link the construct again when needed to distinguish actors or recover the subject after a long excerpt. A pronoun whose referent is unclear requires an explicit subject, but every explanation need not begin with a function name.

The prose before the fence establishes the context and what to observe; the prose after it explains what the shown operations establish and why that matters to the next step. Each side adds to the explanation rather than repeating a complete conclusion, and the prose after the fence never carries a construct's first introduction, what it is and what it is for, which stands above the fence. Read every post-fence sentence with its introduction and source for that connection.
