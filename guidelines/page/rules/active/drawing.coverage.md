# Coverage

The figures of a page, read in page order with nothing but their titles and legends, are the page's outline: a reader who looks only at the figures can say what each subsection establishes and in what order, which is what a skim of the page gives.

Every function the page catalogs has its concept in a figure, the relationship or operation the function establishes, drawn where the subsection explains it: the structure it fills, the transition it drives, the hand-off it takes part in, the range it checks, the bits it reads.

One figure covers the helpers that act on one relationship, and an accessor is covered by the figure of the structure it reads, a validation helper by the figure of the range or state it tests; a function whose concept no figure carries is a gap as real as an undrawn trigger shape, and the subsection that explains it draws one.

The set of figures is no catalog of symbols: each figure depicts a journey or a model under the pattern index, and what the set covers is concepts, not names.

A figure that names no function site, an **unmarked figure** with no marks and no legend, drawn from objects, fields, events and values alone, covers a concept as well as a marked one and costs less: it takes no mark family, needs no legend and no walk paragraph, and can be added after the draft without touching another figure.

Reach coverage with unmarked figures where the subsection's prose and excerpts already name the sites, and with marked figures where the reader must go from the drawing to the code.

To check, list the figures in page order with their titles beside COVERAGE, read the titles alone as an outline against the route paragraph, and for each cataloged function name the figure that carries its concept. A function with none, or a stretch of subsections the outline skips, is the finding.
