# Paragraphs

A paragraph carries one relationship. Follow its actor or object through an operation and its result before changing branch, abstraction or time. Keep each sentence on one explanatory thread; an operation with its necessary conditions and direct result may stay together, and an aside about implementation location, history or another branch takes a separate sentence or paragraph.

Complete the paragraph's main explanation before adding background or history it does not depend on. Keep qualifying conditions beside the claims they limit. A paragraph, its sentences, the runs of paragraphs and a subsection without a block keep the local target and review boundaries; a paragraph past its target length is read for a second relationship.

A shorter paragraph still needs the connections that make its steps intelligible; do not compress separate explanations merely to meet a length target.

Use as many sentences as the paragraph's relationship needs. There is no per-paragraph sentence-count target or limit. Separate independent statements rather than joining them with commas or semicolons to reduce the sentence count. Review each sentence past its word boundary for a change of actor, a second causal step or an aside that belongs in another sentence.

Review these paragraph runs:

- two directly consecutive one-sentence paragraphs, except a recap followed by the subsection's closing paragraph;
- more than three paragraphs in one run; or
- three consecutive paragraphs over 80 words.

Target and review boundaries:

| Unit | Measure | Target | Review candidate | Violation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a sentence | words | 15–22 | over 30 | |
| a `P` | words | 45–80 | over 120 | |
| a DETAILS subsection without a block | words | up to 250 | over 250 | |


For these boundaries, P is a prose paragraph, C a source fence and D a figure fence. Count C as reproduced source lines, excluding provenance and elision lines. Count D as nonblank body lines, including legends. Apply these numerical measurements to DETAILS, including its preamble, rather than to the lead or SUMMARY.

The runs are counted over the prose paragraphs of one subsection in order:

- two directly consecutive one-sentence paragraphs are two prose paragraphs with no block between them;
- a run of paragraphs is the prose paragraphs between two blocks or a block and the subsection's end.
