# Summary

SUMMARY is a map: the model, then the primary journey with its governing invariant or boundary. Every sentence carries one of these four roles. Keep a claim already made in the lead only where it adds a relationship, phase or constraint. Keep the local targets and shapes; the prose beside a table or figure uses the shorter band. Inventories belong in COVERAGE, bit and register breakdowns in section six, and execution, variants and error paths in DETAILS. Identify sentence roles during review.

Target and review boundaries:

| Unit | Measure | Target | Review candidate | Violation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SUMMARY with no table and no figure | words | 80–160 | 161–300 | over 300 |
| SUMMARY beside its table or figure, both sides together | words | 60–140 | 141–220 | over 220 |
| SUMMARY | sentences | 4–6 | 7–9 | over 9 |
| SUMMARY | `P` items | 1–2 | 3 | over 3 |
| a figure in SUMMARY | lines | 8–80 | under 8, over 80 | |

Require SUMMARY. Allow at most one table or one figure, never both, and no source or quotation fence. A table has at most six body rows and four columns. The first and last items are prose.


For these boundaries, P is a prose paragraph, C a source fence and D a figure fence. Count C as reproduced source lines, excluding provenance and elision lines. Count D as nonblank body lines, including legends. Measure the lead or SUMMARY as a whole, as the unit column specifies.
